# Cold Email Subject Rules

## Purpose
This file defines how subject lines should be judged and rewritten for cold emails.

The subject line should support the email, not distract from it.

## Source of Truth
This file must stay aligned with:
- `SKILL.md`
- `references/cold-email-principles.md`
- the active rubric
- the current evaluation result

## Subject Line Goal
A good subject line should:
- feel specific
- feel human
- feel relevant to the recipient
- create enough curiosity to open
- stay short and easy to scan on mobile
- match the body of the email

## What Good Subject Lines Usually Do
Good subject lines often:
- reference a real trigger or context
- sound like they were written for one person
- avoid hype and vague marketing language
- stay low pressure
- hint at the value or reason for reaching out

## What to Reward
Reward subject lines that:
- feel personal or contextual
- connect clearly to the email body
- use concrete language
- are short and readable
- make the email feel worth opening
- sound natural and not over-optimized

## What to Penalize
Penalize subject lines that:
- are generic
- could be sent to anyone
- sound like mass outreach
- use empty curiosity bait
- use buzzwords
- feel AI-generated
- are overly long
- do not match the body of the email

## Common Weak Subject Patterns
Strongly penalize:
- “Quick note”
- “Checking in”
- “No pitch here”
- “Following up”
- “Any plans at X?”
- “Just wanted to reach out”
- vague urgency without context
- fake intimacy
- subject lines that say nothing meaningful

## Subject Line Style Rules
- Keep it short.
- Keep it natural.
- Keep it easy to understand.
- Keep it aligned with the email body.
- Use regular dashes if needed.
- Avoid em-dashes if they make the subject feel artificial.
- Prefer clarity over cleverness unless the cleverness still feels natural.

## Relevance Rules
A subject line is relevant when:
- it reflects the recipient’s context
- it reflects the reason for reaching out
- it helps the recipient immediately understand why the email matters

A subject line is irrelevant when:
- it is too broad
- it sounds copied from a template
- it does not match the actual body content

## Specificity Rules
A subject line is specific when:
- it includes a real detail
- it points to a clear event, context, or reason
- it feels written for one person rather than many

A subject line is not specific when:
- it is generic
- it could apply to almost any outreach message
- it is too vague to create trust

## Low-Friction Rules
The subject line should make opening feel easy.

Good subject lines:
- do not feel pushy
- do not overpromise
- do not feel like clickbait
- do not create unnecessary pressure

## Rewrite Rules for Subject Lines
When rewriting a weak subject line:
1. Keep the meaning aligned with the body.
2. Make it shorter if possible.
3. Remove vague or hype-heavy phrasing.
4. Add a real detail if the body supports it.
5. Make it sound human.
6. Keep it easy to scan on mobile.

## When to Leave the Subject Line Alone
Do not rewrite the subject line if:
- it is already short and clear
- it already matches the body well
- it is already specific enough
- the body is weak, but the subject is acceptable

## Output Expectations
If the subject line needs improvement, the evaluator may return:
- one improved subject line
- a small set of alternatives
- a brief note explaining why the new subject is better

If the subject line is already good, the evaluator should return:
- null
- or a note saying it is already acceptable

## Guardrails
Do not:
- invent subject-line tricks that are not supported by the skill
- replace a decent subject line for no reason
- add fake urgency
- add fake personalization
- overcomplicate a simple subject line
- drift away from the body of the email
