# Cold Email Examples

These examples are original reference cases scored against the 5-point rubric:
- Relevant
- Specific
- Clear
- Human
- Low friction

They are designed to show what strong cold outreach looks like in practice.

---

## Strong Example 1

### Subject
Quick idea for reducing onboarding drop-off

### Body
Hi Sarah,

I noticed your signup flow gets new users into the dashboard very quickly, but there is not much guidance after that first step.

That usually means people are more likely to stall before they reach their first meaningful action.

I mocked up two small onboarding changes after testing the flow for 15 minutes.

If useful, I can send the screenshots over. Worth a quick look?

Best,
Ishan

### Expected Scores
- Relevant: 4
- Specific: 4
- Clear: 5
- Human: 4
- Low friction: 5

### Why this works
- It opens with a direct and personal greeting.
- It references something concrete about the product.
- It explains why the sender is reaching out now.
- The ask is short and easy to answer.
- It sounds like a real person, not a template.

## Strong Example 2

### Subject
Reducing support backlog for billing teams

### Body
Hi Alex,

I saw that your team recently added a self-serve billing flow.

One thing I noticed is that billing questions can pile up fast when the confirmation email does not answer the next obvious question.

I helped another team cut those questions by simplifying the confirmation message and adding one clearer next step.

I can send a short teardown with the exact changes if that would be helpful. Reply yes and I will send it.

Best,
Mina

### Expected Scores
- Relevant: 4
- Specific: 4
- Clear: 5
- Human: 4
- Low friction: 5

### Why this works
- It is tailored to a real workflow concern.
- It gives a believable reason for contacting the person.
- It includes a concrete outcome.
- The request is bounded and easy to accept.
- The tone is calm and conversational.

## Weak Example 1

### Subject
Quick note

### Body
Hey,

I help businesses grow through AI-powered marketing solutions.

I would love to connect and discuss how we can work together.

Please let me know if you have time this week.

Best,
John

### Expected Scores
- Relevant: 1
- Specific: 1
- Clear: 3
- Human: 2
- Low friction: 2

### Why this is weak
- It could be sent to almost anyone.
- It has no real personalization.
- The offer is vague.
- The CTA is soft and unbounded.
- The subject line is generic.

## Weak Example 2

### Subject
Checking in

### Body
Hi there,

I came across your profile and thought I would reach out.

We have a platform that helps teams streamline workflow and improve efficiency.

Would you be open to learning more?

Thanks,
Ryan

### Expected Scores
- Relevant: 1
- Specific: 1
- Clear: 2
- Human: 2
- Low friction: 1

### Why this is weak
- It does not show why the recipient was chosen.
- The value proposition is generic.
- There is no concrete reason to reply.
- The ask is vague and too open-ended.
- It sounds mass-sent.

---

## Patterns to Reward

Good cold emails usually:
- reference something specific about the recipient
- explain why the sender is reaching out now
- use concrete language
- make one clear ask
- sound like a person wrote them
- keep the reply effort small

## Patterns to Penalize

Weak cold emails usually:
- use generic subject lines
- avoid specifics
- sound copy-pasted
- hide the ask
- use vague language
- feel like a broadcast message
- create too much friction to reply
